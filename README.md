# The Version That Stayed

A small repository about loving someone deeply, losing them, and learning how to carry the memory without carrying the relationship forever.

This isn't a case for getting her back. It isn't proof that the relationship should have lasted. It's a record of how much it mattered to me.

## What I loved

I loved having a person.

Someone to tell the little parts of my day to. Someone whose presence made ordinary things feel important. Walking across a small bridge holding hands, looking up at the stars, downloading an app so I could name the constellations — tiny moments that became huge because they were ours.

For a while, I didn't just imagine a girlfriend. I imagined a home, a future, kids, stability, and the feeling that I had finally been chosen.

## What hurt

The ending felt abrupt to me. I didn't understand in the beginning that it was really over, and later I was left with the feeling that someone who had been woven into my everyday life could suddenly become completely absent.

I still catch myself trying to understand it. Sometimes I miss the real person. Sometimes I miss a version of her that existed mostly in hope — the version who stayed, listened, chose me consistently, and made loneliness feel smaller.

## What I know now

The fantasy is not the current person. After enough time apart, I don't really know who she is anymore.

What remains is memory, attachment, grief, and meaning.

I can admit that I loved her without making that love a reason to contact her, wait for her, investigate her life, or keep my own life frozen.

> It was real. It mattered. It hurt me. I learned from it. And it still doesn't belong in my future.

## The goal

I don't want to erase her.

I want the memory to stop asking anything from me.

I want to finish my degree, leave the environments that keep this chapter alive, build a life that feels like mine again, and eventually meet someone I can love without loneliness choosing for me.

Maybe one day this repository will feel less like a wound and more like an old photograph.
